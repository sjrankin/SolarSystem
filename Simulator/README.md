# Solar System Simulator

A Godot 4.7 project that simulates the Sun and the nine classical planets (Mercury to Pluto).

## Running

Open this folder in Godot and press Play (main scene: `scenes/main.tscn`), or run
`godot --path .` from this folder.

## What it does

- Planets move on Keplerian orbits using the JPL J2000 orbital elements (semi-major axis,
  eccentricity, inclination, mean longitude, longitude of perihelion, longitude of the ascending
  node). Kepler's equation is solved each frame, so positions, orbit shapes/tilts and relative
  speeds are the real ones. The simulation starts on today's date.
- Every planet (and the Sun) has a name label that always faces the camera and stays a constant size.
- The Sun and planets are textured (`textures/<Name>.png`, equirectangular). These are procedural
  approximations (ice caps and clouds on Earth, banding and the Great Red Spot on Jupiter, craters on
  Mercury, and so on), generated by `tools/generate_textures.gd`. To use real maps, replace the PNGs
  with files of the same name and re-import; a body without a texture falls back to a flat colour.
- Every planet and the Sun is tilted to its IAU north pole (J2000 RA/Dec in `planet_data.gd`), so
  Earth leans 23.4°, Uranus lies on its side, and so on. The poles are fixed in space, so seasons and
  Saturn's ring tilt change naturally along the orbit. (Venus, Uranus and Pluto spin retrograde; the
  IAU pole is their geographic north, so their tilt is the supplement of the quoted obliquity.)
  Planets and the Sun also spin about those axes at their real (IAU) rotation rates and phases,
  tied to the simulation clock, so Earth turns once per simulated day (Venus, Uranus and Pluto
  spin retrograde). At high simulation speeds the spin looks fast or strobes, as it would on film.
- Moons: Earth's Moon, Jupiter's Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto, and Saturn's Mimas, Enceladus,
  Tethys, Dione, Rhea, Titan and Iapetus; Uranus's Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania and Oberon; and
  Neptune's Triton (retrograde), Proteus and Nereid. Positions come from JPL's mean orbital elements for
  2000-01-01 (`scripts/moon_data.gd`), with apsidal and nodal precession, in each moon's own
  reference plane (the ecliptic for the Moon and Nereid, local Laplace planes for Jupiter's and
  Saturn's, the planet's equator for Uranus's and Neptune's; Nereid's elements are for 2020).
  Triton and Proteus use their observed sidereal periods instead of the rounded JPL values.
  The Uranus/Neptune element values were read from summaries of the JPL page, so treat their
  phases as less certain than the others. Phases are
  within a few degrees of real (mean elements omit short-period perturbations). Moon distances are
  scaled radially about their planet (about planet radius x (1.6 + 0.55 sqrt(real distance / planet
  radius))) so they sit clear of the exaggerated spheres in their real order; labels and orbit
  lines appear when the camera is close to a planet. Moons do not affect the gravity-well grid.
- A fine grid ("gravity wells") sags below the ecliptic. Each body adds a softened 1/r well and the
  wells are summed, so they merge, with saddle points, where bodies are close (e.g. Sun-Jupiter).
  Well shapes are evaluated at true AU separations in either distance mode. Depths are
  exaggerated and compressed for visibility (depth ~ mass^0.2 relative to the Sun), so they show
  ranking and joining, not true proportions. The mesh is adaptive: it follows the camera target and
  covers an area proportional to the camera distance, with extra vertices bunched toward the
  centre, so wells stay smooth as you zoom in; the grid lines (world-fixed) change spacing by
  octaves with a cross-fade. Press G to toggle. See `scripts/gravity_well.gd` and
  `shaders/gravity_well.gdshader`.
- Planet sizes are exaggerated so they are visible; the Sun and planets are not to scale with distance.

## Flight planner

Type a request into the planner panel (press `/` to focus it) such as "fly from Pluto to Mars to Saturn, favor energy over
time", then press Plan. A local language model (Ollama, default `devstral:24b`, changed in the panel's Model box and remembered in
`user://flight_settings.cfg`) turns the text into a route, says whether to favor time or energy, and suggests alternative routes
with extra gravity-assist planets. The model never does the orbital maths: every route is planned by the solver in
`scripts/astro/`, the best one (judged on a common time/energy score) is drawn with its departure, fly-by and arrival dates, and
the model then explains the result. "Fly it" sets the clock to the departure and runs the trip with a spacecraft marker.
With **Follow** on (the switch beside Clear, or F), the camera chases the spacecraft while the flight is in progress, from
departure to arrival: it glides in low and close behind it, just above the plane of the solar system, looking the way it is going,
and turns as the path curves. Before departure it waits, and on arrival it stops where it is. Dragging swings the view around the
spacecraft, zoom and moving still work, and double-click comes back to the chase view. Check with
`godot --headless --path . --script res://test/check_follow.gd`.

- Each leg is a Lambert transfer between the planets' real positions (`lambert.gd`), found by searching departure dates and flight
  times for the cheapest plan (`flight_planner.gd`: a dynamic programme over a time grid, then a local refinement of the dates).
- Intermediate planets are gravity assists (`flyby.gd`): the planet's gravity bends the path for free up to the limit set by its
  mass and the closest allowed pass (its radius plus a margin); whatever the planet cannot do costs an engine burn. Departure and
  arrival are burns from / into a low circular orbit, whose size depends on the planet's gravity well (`Astro.park_radius_au`).
- The cost is `(1 - w) * delta-v + w * time` (each scaled by the route's textbook transfer), where `w` is the "time weight"
  from the request ("favor time over energy" is high, "save fuel" low), or the Energy/Time slider when the request says nothing.
  Limits such as "within 10 years" or "under 20 km/s" are honoured.
- The model is optional. Before each plan the panel checks (in at most 3 seconds) that Ollama is running and has the model. If it
  is not running, the model is not installed, the answer is slow or can't be used, or you press Skip model (the Plan button while
  it waits), the plain reader (`flight_request.gd`) takes over. It still finds the planets, the preference and the limits in the
  text, without the extra routes or the explanation, and the status line says why. Check with
  `godot --headless --path . --script res://test/check_llm_offline.gd` (no Ollama needed). When the simulator's window closes, it asks Ollama to unload
  any model it used (keep_alive 0, at most 2 seconds), so the memory is freed at once; Ollama itself keeps running. Check with
  `godot --headless --path . --script res://test/check_unload.gd`. The server address is `127.0.0.1` because Godot tries the IPv6 address of `localhost`, which
  Ollama does not listen on.
- Limits of the model: Keplerian planets, patched conics, no multi-revolution transfers, an assist is instantaneous (no waiting in
  orbit between arriving and leaving), and planet masses include their moons.

## Clock

The Clock panel (top right) shows the simulation date and controls time: Freeze / Run, Today, Real time (the current date and
time, running at one second per second and kept in step with the computer's clock until you change the clock), a speed menu
from 1 second to 1000 years per second (with ◀◀ / ▶▶ to halve or double), and a box to set any date (`YYYY-MM-DD` or `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`, UTC).
Setting a date moves every planet and moon there at once, even while frozen. The JPL elements are most accurate from 1800 to
2050. "Fly it" in the flight planner runs the clock at the flight's own speed and, when the spacecraft arrives, puts the speed
back to what it was before (choosing a speed or a date during the flight keeps your choice instead). Check with
`godot --headless --path . --script res://test/check_clock.gd`.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Left-drag | Rotate the view |
| Right-drag, middle-drag, Option/Shift + left-drag | Move (pan) the view |
| Mouse wheel / trackpad scroll / pinch | Zoom |
| Double-click | Smoothly restore the original view |
| Space | Pause / resume |
| `+` / `-` (or Up / Down) | Double / halve simulation speed (default 10 days per second; 1 second to 1000 years per second) |
| L | Toggle compressed (sqrt) and linear distances |
| G | Show / hide the gravity-well grid |
| T | Jump back to today's date |
| R | Real time: the current date and time, at one second per second |
| F | Follow the spacecraft on / off |

Compressed distances (default) scale each orbit's radius by the square root of its size in AU so the
inner planets stay readable next to Pluto; directions, and so the angular positions, are unchanged.

## Layout

- `scripts/planet_data.gd` – orbital elements and visual properties.
- `scripts/orbit_math.gd` – Kepler solver and position/scale maths.
- `scripts/planet.gd` – planet node (rotating sphere, Saturn's rings, label, orbit points, moons).
- `scripts/moon.gd`, `scripts/moon_data.gd` – moon nodes and JPL orbital elements.
- `scripts/orbit_camera.gd` – rotate / pan / zoom / double-click reset camera.
- `scripts/gravity_well.gd`, `shaders/gravity_well.gdshader` – gravity-well grid.
- `scripts/solar_system.gd` – scene setup, clock, HUD.
- `scripts/astro/` – `astro.gd` (units, planet gravity), `ephemeris.gd`, `lambert.gd`, `flyby.gd`, `flight_planner.gd`.
- `scripts/flight/` – `ollama_client.gd`, `flight_request.gd` (prompts, checking the model's answers), `flight_ui.gd`
  (panel), `flight_path.gd` (drawing), `flight_settings.gd`.
- `test/check_flight.gd` – headless checks of the solver and request parsing:
  `godot --headless --path . --script res://test/check_flight.gd`. `test/check_llm.gd` and `test/check_flight_ui.gd` need a
  running Ollama; `test/screenshot.gd` needs a window.
- `test/check_positions.gd` – headless checks:
  `godot --headless --path . --script res://test/check_positions.gd`
